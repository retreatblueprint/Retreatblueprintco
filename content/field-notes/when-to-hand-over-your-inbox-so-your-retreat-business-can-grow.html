The short answer

  1. When: Hand over the inbox when answering messages competes with hosting guests, leading your team and working on growth.
  2. What goes first: Routine inquiries, availability and booking logistics, and supplier confirmations.
  3. What you keep: Pricing exceptions, partnership decisions and guest escalations.
  4. Who takes it: A concierge ops service, which puts a trained administrative assistant on your inbox, working from your SOPs.
  5. How: Reply templates, an FAQ sheet and a brief SOP, with clear rules for what reaches you.

Why the inbox becomes the ceiling

In a 2023 Time etc survey of 251 US entrepreneurs, more than a third of the working week, 36%, went to small administrative tasks.1

In a retreat business, much of that time sits in the inbox.

Early on, owning it is the right call. You learn what guests ask, how they decide and what makes them book.

The trouble starts at the next stage. The inbox keeps taking your best hours, and nobody is in the seat running growth.

Four signs it is time

  1. Your sharpest hours go to replies. Programs, pricing and partnerships wait for whatever is left of the day.
  2. Retreat weeks compete with the inbox. On site, your attention belongs with your guests, and messages keep arriving.
  3. Reply time varies with your workload. Messages get answered fastest on calm days and wait on retreat days.
  4. Growth work keeps slipping. Next season's programs, forecasting and strategy sit behind the inbox.

What it looks like in practice

A retreat owner I worked with in Costa Rica ran a specialty program that needed him hands-on, from setup to safety oversight.

During retreat weeks, something had to give. Either the administration fell behind or the experience suffered.

He hired a part-time administrative assistant to manage the inbox and retreat coordination.

Now he is fully present for the experience, and the guest communication keeps moving.

What to hand over, and what to keep

Hand over first

  • Availability and booking inquiries
  • Pre-arrival logistics and guest coordination
  • Routine supplier confirmations
  • Frequently asked questions

Keep

  • Pricing exceptions and policy decisions
  • Partnership and vendor negotiations
  • Safety concerns and guest complaints

A handover needs standards, not just access

The handover works when the person behind the inbox has the standards to answer well.

In Altek's 2026 study of 2 million emails across 48 hotels, only 1 in 8 replies gave guests what they needed to book. Booking rates ranged from 19% to 55% between properties.2

The strongest results came from complete, action-oriented replies and follow-up.

A team that answers fully and with confidence gives guests a better experience from the first message. That is what earns the second booking.

How to hand it over

  1. Audit one week. Log every message you answer and sort it: routine, needs judgment, or yours alone.
  2. Write the standards. Build reply templates and an FAQ sheet, internal and external, plus a brief SOP for each routine inquiry type.
  3. Set escalation rules. Define what reaches you and what the team resolves without you.
  4. Review weekly for a month. Read a sample of replies and refine the templates.

Who takes it

A concierge ops service gives you an administrative assistant for the inbox and coordination, backed by your templates, FAQ sheet and SOPs.

They answer across email, WhatsApp and social channels, and they cover retreat weeks and your days off.

Management stays with you. The team answers, and you decide.

The goal is not an empty inbox. It is an owner free to lead the business and fully present for the experience.
