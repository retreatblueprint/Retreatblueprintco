The short answer

  1. What to measure: repeat guests, repeat retreat leaders, new reviews, total profit, occupancy, revenue per retreat and inquiry-to-booking rate.
  2. When: pull the numbers in the first half of December, while the year's bookings and payments are still easy to find.
  3. What the report should say: what worked, what did not, and the three things you will measure every month next year.

Why a year-end review beats a feeling

A full calendar does not always mean a healthy year. The numbers show where the money and the effort actually went.

In the retreats I work with, the owners who plan best next season are the ones who can say, in numbers, what this season did. They are also the ones who prepare for high season weeks ahead.

Repeat guests and repeat retreat leaders

Retention is the quietest number in a retreat center and one of the most useful. How many of this year's guests will book again next year?

If you host outside leaders, count them separately. A leader who returns brings a group with them, and a leader who does not is a gap in next year's calendar.

Reviews and profit

Count the new reviews you gained this year, and note which retreats earned them. Reviews are what the next guest reads before they inquire.

Then the number that decides everything else: total profit for the year. Take revenue, subtract every cost, and write down what is left. If it takes more than an afternoon, that is the first thing to fix in your systems.

{{photo:photo-1648201637025-1c77b9be3013|A calculator and a pen resting on a sheet of paper|Revenue minus every cost: the one sum worth doing before the year closes.}}

Occupancy, revenue per retreat and inquiry-to-booking rate

Occupancy shows you the quiet months to plan for. Revenue per retreat shows which programs carry the year.

Inquiry-to-booking rate shows what happens between the first message and the deposit. When it drops, the cause is usually slow replies, unclear pricing or no follow-up, all fixable.

What your year-end report should say

Your report fits on one page and has three parts.

  1. Numbers. The seven above, with last year's figure beside each where you have it.
  2. Systems. Where your bookings, payments, inquiries and reviews are tracked today, and which of them you had to dig for.
  3. Team. Who owns each number, and who reviews the report with you every month.

The report should end with the three numbers you will check monthly next year. A number you review once a year can only explain the past. A number you review monthly changes the season.

Where to start this week

Export your bookings and payments now, while the year is fresh. By mid-December, put the seven numbers in one sheet. On December 31, take a snapshot of your review counts and write the page.
