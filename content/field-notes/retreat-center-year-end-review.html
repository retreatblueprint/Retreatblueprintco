The short answer

  1. What to measure: repeat guests, retreat leaders (past and returning), new reviews, total profit, occupancy, retreat types, revenue per retreat and inquiry-to-booking rate.
  2. When: pull the numbers in the first half of December, while the year's bookings and payments are still easy to find.
  3. What the report should say: what worked, what did not, and the three things you will measure every month next year.

Why a year-end review beats a feeling

A full calendar does not always mean a healthy year. The numbers show where the money and the effort actually went.

In the retreats I work with, the owners who plan best next season are the ones who can say, in numbers, what this season did. They are also the ones who prepare for high season weeks ahead.

Repeat guests and retreat leaders

Retention is the quietest number in a retreat center and one of the most useful. How many of this year's guests will book again next year?

If you host outside leaders, count them separately: every leader who has booked with you, in the past or for next year, and who has booked again. A leader who returns brings a group with them, and a leader who does not is a gap in next year's calendar.

Reviews and profit

Count the new reviews you gained this year on every platform, including social media posts that use your hashtags, and note which retreats earned them. Reviews are what the next guest reads before they inquire.

Then the number that tells you whether the center is financially healthy: total profit for the year. Take revenue, subtract every cost, and write down what is left. If it takes more than an afternoon, that is the first thing to fix in your systems.

Occupancy, retreat types, revenue and inquiries

Occupancy shows you the quiet months to plan for.

Retreat types show which niche is gaining the most popularity at your venue, so you know where selling is already easy and where you need to work harder to sell more. Revenue per retreat, grouped by type, shows which types are the most profitable to host, so you can look for more leaders and client profiles like them.

Inquiry-to-booking rate shows whether your replies and follow-up turn interest into bookings. A low rate means there is room to improve. A high rate means that part is working.

What your year-end report should say

Your report fits on one page and has three parts.

  1. Numbers. The eight above, with last year's figure beside each where you have it.
  2. Systems. Where your bookings, payments, inquiries and reviews are tracked today, and which of them you had to dig for.
  3. Team. Who owns each number, and who reviews the report with you every month.

The report should end with the three numbers you will check monthly next year. A number you review once a year can only explain the past. A number you review monthly changes the season.

Where to start this week

Export your bookings and payments now, while the year is fresh. By mid-December, put the eight numbers in one sheet. On December 31, take a snapshot of your review counts and write the page.
