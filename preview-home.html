Where it gets heavy

The experience is your gift. The admin shouldn't be your full-time job.

Venue owners carry a lot: guests, retreat leaders, the team, the money. The heavy part usually isn't the work itself. It's that so much of it lives in one person's head, and that never switches off.

Where it gets heavy

What it can feel like instead

Everything runs through you

Every follow-up, reminder and payment chase waits for you, so the business can only move as fast as you can, and a break feels impossible.

→

Systems that keep moving when you step away

The guest journey runs on its own, from confirmation to follow-up, so the business no longer depends on how much you can personally hold.

Inquiries wait while you're hosting

An inquiry comes in during a retreat, and by the time there is a moment to reply, the guest has booked somewhere else.

→

Every inquiry answered and followed up

Replies go out quickly with a well-timed follow-up, so interested guests stay warm and book.

Every retreat starts from scratch

Without written steps and clear handoffs, each retreat depends on who is holding it that week, and you fill the gaps.

→

A team that knows what to do

Clear steps for before, during and after each retreat, so every retreat runs well, whoever is on shift.

Small details get lost between teams

A missing detail before arrival, a dietary need that never reaches the kitchen. Small things behind the scenes become the moments guests remember.

→

Guests who feel held from first email to final review

Fast, warm replies, clear pre-arrival information, needs passed to the right person, and a thoughtful follow-up after they leave. The systems stay invisible; the care is what guests notice.

Why retreats

I work in this industry because it has my soul

My name is Shila Kandiah. I have spent 10 years in operations management across healthcare, wellness and hospitality, helping businesses with process improvement, operations, and strategy. That mix matters in retreat work, where guest care, dietary and health intake, privacy and safety sit right next to bookings and budgets. I wanted to take everything I had built professionally and point it at something that actually mattered to me personally.

I have CPTSD and I am on my own healing journey of genuinely taking care of mind, body, and soul. Safe spaces that make room for wellness and healing are deeply important to me. I know what it means to find a place that holds you.

So right now I am taking my expertise toward my personal journey and partnering with retreats to help them grow, be seen, and serve the people who really need them. This is the work that has my whole heart.

When a retreat grows, more people heal. That is the business I want to help build.
